Soar Boar is a sixty-second word-chaining game where players change one letter at a time to climb from one word to the next. I designed and shipped it end-to-end as the sole designer, partnering with Claude as my engineer to translate every decision into production code — a working test of how far a designer can take a product without writing the code themselves.

I developed the brand system, mascot illustration family, cross-mode product design, accessibility tooling, and the underlying game mechanics — including the word-pair difficulty grading that surfaces ladders that are solvable but not obvious. I am continuing to extend the game with new modes and mascots, and exploring how the same coaching layer could power lightly-guided puzzles in other categories.

The three modes — Soar Boar (pig), Soy Boy (bean), and This That (a hair-flipping pig) — share a single visual language but each carries its own mood, palette, and personality. Accessibility was baked into the foundation rather than retrofitted: every accent meets minimum WCAG contrast against its background, and a built-in checker makes the math visible to anyone tuning a color. The result is a game that is bright, opinionated, and accessible by default — a tiny, warm world that takes its players seriously while keeping the math at the door.
